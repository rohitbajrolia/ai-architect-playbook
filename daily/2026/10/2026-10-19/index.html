    2) GenAI¶

    Efficient Sparse Models in Production¶

    Architectural Implication

    • [COST] Infra, Hardware & Cost — Sparse activation layers cut GPU usage costs but require new monitoring APIs to detect model dropouts in underwriting model pipelines.
    • [REL] Reliability & Evaluation — Regression testing must include checks for sparse layer stability to avoid unexpected failures impacting claims decision workflows.
    • [OPS] Product & Operating Model — Operations teams need training on deploying and tuning sparse models in regulated environments with fallback to dense models for compliance auditing.

    Open questions - How does sparse activation impact explainability for model decisions tied to regulatory compliance?

    Enhanced Control Layers for Compliance¶

    Architectural Implication

    • [AGENT] Agents & Orchestration — Control layers must be integrated into claims triage agents to enforce hard stops on policy interpretation beyond regulatory bounds.
    • [GOV] Security, Privacy & Governance — Auditable logs of control layer overrides are essential for compliance reporting under NAIC’s new AI governance guidelines.
    • [DATA] Data, RAG & Knowledge — Control layer inputs require contextual data tagging to ensure compliance constraints adapt dynamically to claim types and jurisdictions.

    3) Agentic AI¶

    Reliable Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)¶

    Architectural Implication

    • [DATA] Data, RAG & Knowledge — Tight integration of retrieval chains reduces hallucination risk in agent-assisted customer support for policy servicing.
    • [REL] Reliability & Evaluation — Continuous eval pipelines must measure hallucination rates and retrieval freshness to maintain SLA for response accuracy.
    • [OPS] Product & Operating Model — Teams must maintain retrieval source ranking and freshness metadata to avoid stale or incorrect information in underwriting agent responses.

    4) AI Radar¶

    Embedding Models for Fraud Detection¶

    Architectural Implication

    • [COST] Infra, Hardware & Cost — Faster embedding extraction models reduce inference latency but require revisiting batch processing design to handle spikes during fraud alerts.
    • [REL] Reliability & Evaluation — Embedding drift detection needs embedding-specific metrics to avoid silent degradation in fraud detection accuracy.
    • [GOV] Security, Privacy & Governance — Embedding artifacts must comply with privacy laws, especially under GDPR for EU policyholders’ data in fraud pipelines.

    5) CTO Brief¶

    • Sparse activation models reduce inference costs but require monitoring for dropout issues in underwriting.
    • Fine-grained control layers on agents enhance regulatory compliance, needing comprehensive audit logs.
    • Improved RAG integration methods lower hallucinations, critical for policy servicing chatbots under compliance.

    6) Rohit's Notes¶

    • Sparse models bring efficiency but I need to re-check the operational impact on compliance audits this week.

    7) Design Drill¶

    Scenario: Implementing an AI-assisted claims triage system for a US insurer needing rapid underwriting decision support with compliance audit trails.

    Constraints: - Must comply with NAIC’s updated AI governance framework. - Integration with legacy policy servicing databases requiring low-latency retrieval. - Ensure full audit logs of all AI decisions for regulatory review.

    Guiding questions: - How to architect sparse model deployments to balance cost and regulatory auditing needs? - What control layers or human-in-the-loop gates are essential for claims triage autonomy? - How to integrate retrieval freshness indicators to reduce hallucinations in triage explanations? - What logging standards ensure full compliance with NAIC auditability mandates? - How to handle fallback safely if the sparse model triggers confidence drops during inference?
